## Source Summary

Indeed presents STAR as a practical answer structure for behavioral and situational interview questions. STAR stands for situation, task, action, and result, with the action section carrying the most weight because it shows how the candidate behaved.

## Key Notes

### Situation

- Give concise context for a specific real scenario.
- Include only the details needed to understand the challenge.

### Task

- Describe the responsibility, target, or problem to solve.
- Keep this section short and clear.

### Action

- Explain the steps personally taken by the candidate.
- Prefer "I" over "we" so the interviewer can see individual contribution.
- This is usually the most important part of the answer.

### Result

- State the outcome, impact, or measurable improvement.
- If possible, include metrics, decisions influenced, or lessons learned.

## Interview-Relevant Takeaways

- Good STAR answers are concrete, chronological, and outcome-oriented.
- Behavioural evaluators can use STAR coverage as a lightweight scoring lens.
- Missing or vague "Action" sections are a common weakness in candidate responses.
