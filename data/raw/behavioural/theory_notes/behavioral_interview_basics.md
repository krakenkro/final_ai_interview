## Source Summary

The guide explains what behavioral interview questions are, why employers ask them, and how candidates should prepare. The core idea is that past behavior is used as evidence for future performance, so answers should be concrete, relevant to the role, and structured around real situations.

## Key Notes

### What Interviewers Evaluate

- How the candidate behaved in real situations.
- Whether past actions suggest readiness for the responsibilities of the role.
- Skills such as communication, ownership, decision-making, and learning from challenges.

### How To Prepare

- Study the job description and infer the behaviors being tested.
- Prepare examples that match role expectations.
- Use concrete stories rather than generic statements.

### How To Answer

- Tie the answer to the context of the role.
- Focus on what the candidate actually did, not only what the team did.
- Show the outcome and what was learned.

### Common Themes

- Conflict resolution
- Ownership
- Handling failure
- Prioritization under pressure
- Working with teammates or stakeholders

## Interview-Relevant Takeaways

- Strong behavioural answers are specific, role-relevant, and reflective.
- Weak answers stay abstract, avoid accountability, or omit result and learning.
- A good evaluator rubric should check clarity, ownership, action quality, and reflection depth.
